## The story in one minute

You read an old legend about **Vesper House**, a home for gifted children on **Morrow Isle**. On one October evening in 1899,
the children and their headmistress vanished. You go to the island, and at dusk you find a door that leads into that very day,
which has been repeating for 127 years.

Every evening, a thief called **the Magpie** comes up the path with his invisible **Haunters** to steal the children's gifts.
Every evening, just before he reaches the house, the headmistress, **Miss Vesper**, turns into a bird and rewinds the day to
morning. It has worked for 127 years. Then *you* arrive, the first new thing the Magpie has seen in all that time, and he
starts to remember. Each evening he gets closer.

You have three days. Explore the house, help the children, build the **Seeing Spectacles** so you can see the Haunters, and
prepare the house for the one night it has never had. Defeat the Magpie, and the children finally get to see tomorrow.

## The names (and why)

| Name | Who or what | Why the name |
|---|---|---|
| **Morrow Isle** | The island | "Morrow" is an old word for *tomorrow*. It's the island where tomorrow never comes. |
| **Vesper House** | The children's home | "Vesper" means *evening*. Evening is when everything happens here. |
| **Miss Vesper** | The headmistress | The house is named after her. Every evening she turns into a nightjar, a bird that flies at dusk, and rewinds the day. |
| **The Magpie** | The villain | Magpies steal shiny things. He steals *gifts*, and he dresses in black and white like the bird. |
| **The Haunters** | His invisible hounds | They *haunt* gifted children: they circle, sniff out gifts and snatch them. |
| **Clem Rouse**, 16 | The eldest | Her gift: she can *rouse* objects (wake them up) to do their job, such as making a door slam or the stairs fold. |
| **Echo Hart**, 12 | The listener | Her gift: she hears *echoes* of the past by putting her ear to a wall or an object. |
| **Ezra Fogg**, 13 | The showman | His gift: he breathes *fog*. Invisible things show up as outlines in it. |
| **Silas Ironside**, 14 | The grump | His gift: he's a living magnet who can pull *iron* to him from across a room. |
| **Ada Glass**, 9 | The girl in the mirrors | Her gift: she lives inside the house's mirrors (*looking-glass*) and can travel from one to another. |
| **Pip Little**, 7 | The youngest | His gift: the Haunters can't see him. Sadly, people tend to overlook him too. He's *little* and easy to miss. |
| **The Seeing Spectacles** | What you build | Glasses that let you see the Haunters. |

## The three rules of the loop

1. **Every evening, just before the Magpie reaches the house, Miss Vesper rewinds the day.** She turns into a nightjar, flies
   one circle around the house, and everything goes back to 7 o'clock that morning: Tuesday, 17 October 1899.
2. **The household remembers. The rest of the island doesn't.** The villagers, and the Magpie himself, live the same day fresh
   every time, saying and doing the same things. Or they did, until you came.
3. **You're from outside the loop, so you remember everything, and whatever is in your satchel stays with you.** That's how you
   can gather what you need across several days. And because the day repeats, anything you miss will be there again tomorrow
   morning.

One more fact everyone in the house knows: **the Haunters only hunt at dusk and after dark.** In daylight, the Magpie is just a
well-dressed gentleman waiting at the village inn.

## The story

### Prologue: The Legend

In a second-hand bookshop you find *Legends of Morrow Isle*. One chapter tells of **Vesper House**, a home for gifted children
on a cold northern island. On the evening of **Tuesday, 17 October 1899**, the children and their headmistress vanished. The
islanders say the house still appears at dusk to anyone who goes looking.

You go looking. The present-day island is quiet: a ferry, a pub, and a one-room museum. In a glass case lies the house's old
**guestbook**, saved from the ruins. The last entry is dated 17 October 1899.

**It's your name, in your handwriting.**

At dusk, on the cliff where the house once stood, an old wooden door stands alone in the grass. You knock: *tap, tap, boom.* It
opens onto a bright morning and a house that shouldn't exist.

### Day One: The Stranger

It's 7 o'clock on Tuesday, 17 October 1899, the same morning it has been for 127 years.

The six children haven't met anyone new in all that time, and they're thrilled. Miss Vesper is not. *"If you found the door,"*
she says, *"so could someone else."* House rules say every guest signs the guestbook, so you sign it. You realize it's the same
book you saw in the museum.

You explore the house and meet everyone. Each child has a small problem you can help with, and helping them shows you their
gift:

- **Clem** has never been trusted to do anything on her own. Help her rouse the creaky old dumbwaiter and she proves she can.
- **Echo** can't sleep, because the house's memories are too loud. Make her a pair of earmuffs and she can pick out one sound at a time.
- **Ezra** puts on a fog show that nobody ever watches. Watch it, and when he takes his bow, his fog clings to the outline of
  something standing outside the window that you can't otherwise see.
- **Silas** can't eat a meal without the cutlery flying at him. Carve him a wooden spoon.
- **Ada** hasn't been hugged since she disappeared into the mirrors. She can only step halfway out where two mirrors face each
  other. Find the second mirror.
- **Pip** keeps getting left out because everyone forgets he's there. Notice him.

Bit by bit you learn the truth. The Magpie collects gifts, and his Haunters snatch them from children. He keeps every stolen gift
glowing in a jar in his black-and-white cabinet. In 1899 he came for Vesper House, and he's been coming every evening since.

**The evening.** The household gathers at the windows. The children know exactly what's coming, because they've watched it
every evening for 127 years. Fog rolls in off the sea. Dogs that aren't there begin to howl. A black-and-white carriage comes up
the coast road. A tall man in a black-and-white coat steps out and walks up the path, reading from a little notebook: *"One fog.
One magnet. One looking-glass…"* Ezra mouths the words along with him.

As the Magpie reaches the gate, Miss Vesper climbs onto the roof and turns into a nightjar. While she flies her circle, the
children clap their rhyme. Then the fog runs backwards, the carriage rolls back down the road, and the sun climbs back up the sky.
It's morning again.

But just before the world rewinds, the Magpie looks up at the window, straight at **you**, the one thing that has never been
there before. And he smiles.

### Day Two: He Remembers

It's morning again: the same breakfast, the same rain shower at eleven. Your name has vanished from the guestbook, because
nothing sticks here. But on the doorstep lies something new: **a single black-and-white feather.**

Miss Vesper goes pale. *"He's never left anything before. He remembers."* For 127 years the Magpie forgot every evening. Seeing
you snagged in his memory like a thorn, and now he remembers every attempt, and he'll come back smarter each time.

Hiding won't work much longer. To fight the Haunters, the household needs to *see* them, and only Miss Vesper can. Her old
seeing spectacles were smashed on the first evening, back in 1899. So your main quest begins: **build new Seeing
Spectacles.** The parts come from all over the house and the 1899 village, and the children you've helped are the ones who can
help you find them (see below). In the village you also spot the Magpie by daylight, sitting by the fire at the inn. He raises
his glass to you.

The first time you put the spectacles on, you see the Haunters at last: tall, thin, hound-shaped things made of smoke, pressed
against the windows and sniffing.

**The evening.** The Magpie comes early. He doesn't stop at the gate. He walks straight up to the front door, knocks three
times, and says your name. *"Hello again."*

Miss Vesper takes off just in time, but a Haunter catches her wing. The day rewinds, and she lands hurt. *"I have one more in
me,"* she says quietly. *"Maybe."*

### Day Three: The Last Morning

Miss Vesper decides that the hiding is over. Tonight they won't rewind. Tonight the household stops the Magpie for good. If it
goes wrong, she'll spend her last rewind to give you one more try at this morning.

So you prepare. Each child's gift becomes part of the plan (see below). You gather what you need, make your weapons and
defenses, and talk to everyone, because every child you've helped is a child who can help tonight. When you're ready, you ring
the dinner bell. **Ring it too early and you may not be ready.**

### The Final Night

For the first time in 127 years, night falls on Vesper House. The children see stars for the very first time, and for a moment
nobody says anything. Then the fog comes.

- **The garden.** Haunters pour over the wall. With your spectacles you can see them, Ezra's fog shows them to everyone, and
  the iron line you prepared with Silas holds them back.
- **The front door.** The Magpie knocks. Clem rouses the house: the door refuses to open, and the shutters slam.
- **Inside.** Some Haunters slip in through the cracks. Echo hears where the next one is coming from. Ada pulls the little ones
  to safety through the mirrors.
- **The Magpie himself.** He walks in, wearing the stolen gifts of children from years past. He can turn to smoke and throw his
  voice. His real strength is in his **cabinet** of glowing jars, back on his carriage. While you keep him busy, **Pip**, whom
  the Haunters can't see, slips out to the carriage with the key you stole on Day Two. Pip opens the cabinet, and **every stolen
  gift flies home.** Without them, the Magpie is just an ordinary old man in a black-and-white coat. His Haunters, who only ever
  followed the scent of gifts, scatter into the fog and are gone.

How well each part goes depends on what you prepared. If a Haunter catches a child, it snatches their gift. **If the Magpie
reaches Miss Vesper, or takes too many gifts, the night is lost.** With the last of her strength, Miss Vesper rewinds to the
morning of Day Three. You keep everything you carried and everything you learned, so you can see what you missed and try again.

### Endings

- **The good ending.** You defeat the Magpie and break open his cabinet. Every gift goes home: the children's, and those of
  children from long ago and far away.
- **How good depends on you.** In the epilogue, every child you helped gets their own happy moment. Any child you never got to
  know simply walks off into their future, and you never find out what became of them. The more you explored, the fuller the
  ending.
- **Losing.** If the night is lost, Miss Vesper's last rewind sends you back to the morning of Day Three to try again.

### Epilogue: Tomorrow

The sun rises, and for the first time in 127 years **it's Wednesday.** The children run down to the village, laughing, into a day
nobody has ever seen.

The door back to your own time is still standing, but only until the sun is fully up. Before you go, Miss Vesper hands you the
guestbook. *"Sign it again. This time it will stay."* You write your name, and you realize this is the signature you saw in the
museum.

You step back through the door into the present. In the museum, the guestbook lies open to your name. And the old legend book
has changed. The chapter no longer ends with the children vanishing. Now it ends: *"On Wednesday, 18 October 1899, six children
and their headmistress walked down into the village for the first time anyone could remember. They lived long, ordinary,
extraordinary lives."*

## How the game plays (the short version)

- **Explore and talk.** It's a classic point-and-click: walk around the house and the 1899 village, look at everything, and talk
  to everyone.
- **Collect and combine.** Pick things up, combine them, and give them to the right person.
- **The day repeats, and that helps.** You can learn the villagers' routines (who's where, and when) and use them in puzzles,
  like stealing the cabinet key from the Magpie's coachman when he stops at the inn.
- **Build the Seeing Spectacles,** then **prepare for the final night**.
- **You can lose,** but it never costs you hours: you go back to the last morning with everything you had.
- **Comic peril.** Silly, eerie little deaths along the way (stepping in front of the rouse-happy dumbwaiter, licking a frosty
  window), each followed by an instant **Try Again**.
- **Fair puzzles.** Every solution is hinted at least twice, and you can never get permanently stuck.

## The Seeing Spectacles

Each part comes from a different place or person, so building them takes you all over the house and island:

- **The frames.** Miss Vesper's broken spectacles. **Echo** hears where the pieces were swept up in 1899.
- **The lens glass.** A sliver of mirror from the other side of the glass, which **Ada** brings back for you.
- **Something to show the invisible.** A breath of **Ezra's** fog, sealed between the lenses.
- **A way to hold it together.** Tiny iron screws only **Silas** can handle.
- **A reason to trust you.** A nightjar feather from **Miss Vesper**, to "wake" the lenses. She only gives it once she trusts you.

## What each child does on the final night

| Child | Their part in the plan | If you didn't help them… |
|---|---|---|
| **Clem** | Rouses the house: doors slam, shutters close, stairs fold. | The front door is easier to break. |
| **Echo** | Hears where each Haunter is coming from. | You're guessing in the dark. |
| **Ezra** | Fog that makes the Haunters visible to everyone. | Only you can see them, through your spectacles. |
| **Silas** | Pulls iron into a line the Haunters can't cross. | The garden falls fast. |
| **Ada** | Moves the little ones to safety through the mirrors. | Someone is left exposed. |
| **Pip** | Sneaks to the Magpie's cabinet with the key. | You'll need another way to open it, and it's much harder. |

## The rhyme

The children clap it in the hall every evening while Miss Vesper flies: two claps and a stamp, just like the knock on the door.

> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Magpie's coming, grab a broom!*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom bay,*
> *Wind it back to yesterday.*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Good morning, Morrow! Make some room!*

After the final night, they change the last line: *"Here comes Wednesday! Make some room!"*
