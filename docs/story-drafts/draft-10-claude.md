## The story in one minute

A visitor reads about six children who vanished from Vesper House in 1899 and follows an old drawing to a door on the cliffs of Morrow Isle. On the other side, the children are still living the day they disappeared. At dusk the visitor follows their headmistress and watches her turn into a bird and rewind Tuesday just before the Magpie and his invisible Haunters can steal the children's gifts.

But that first evening, the visitor accidentally leaves the side door open, and the Magpie gets one foot inside the house as the day rewinds. The next morning he remembers what he saw. When he comes back early that evening, Miss Vesper barely finishes her flight. The visitor and the children build glasses to see the Haunters, work out where the Magpie keeps the gifts he's stolen, and prepare the house for one last night. If they can stop him, the children can finally choose to see Wednesday.

## Prologue — A door in the grass

In a second-hand copy of *Legends of Morrow Isle* you find a loose notebook page in a neat, old-fashioned hand, signed *V.* It has a drawing of a door standing by itself in the grass, and a note:

> Two taps on the wood, one stamp on the stone. The children get frightened when anyone tries the handle.

You take the ferry to the island. Above the cliffs, the two chips in the wood and a heel mark in the sill match the drawing. You knock. A warm hallway appears on the other side. A girl on a ladder peers past you.

> **Clem:** “If you've brought the hinge, we only needed it a hundred years ago.”

Miss Vesper takes you in and has you sign the guestbook. She gives you the tour herself until a sound at the side door makes her go quiet. She closes it and asks you to leave the bolt down. The side door's hinge is broken. A new one was ordered in 1899 and is due on Wednesday, which, as Clem points out, never comes.

## First Tuesday — Follow Miss Vesper

You meet the children by getting involved. Clem wakes up a stubborn dumbwaiter and makes it land on the right floor. Echo can hear the last person who repaired it. Silas pulls a dropped iron spoon across the table. Ezra makes fog for a show that mostly consists of waiting for applause. Ada waves from inside a mirror. Pip holds his breath and slips out of a headcount, because while Pip holds his breath, nobody notices him. You notice anyway, and he's delighted.

As the light goes, Ezra's fog catches around the shape of a dog outside the window. The children call the invisible things **Haunters**. They gather in the hall and start a rhyme with the same two taps and stamp you used on the cliff. Miss Vesper leaves her tea and heads upstairs. Follow the painted wings to the roof hatch. Through the slats you see her coat drop and a nightjar rise into the sky.

When the wind slams the hatch shut, you take the side door to see the rest of her flight from the garden. The old hinge catches and the door fails to close behind you. Through the fog you see a man in a black-and-white coat leave a carriage and hurry across the yard. In the hall Clem calls out, “Pip, bring your peg. We're keeping your place.” The man plants a boot past the sill and turns toward her voice.

Miss Vesper lands before he can reach the children. The fog runs backward into the sea. A cup that fell off the kitchen table jumps back into place. You open your eyes to the same breakfast, with Ezra halfway through the same sentence.

Only then does Miss Vesper explain why she flies. She saw what this man had done to a child and rewound the afternoon before he could reach the others. Every evening since, the flight has put the children out of his reach. People inside the house at the moment of the rewind remember it, which is why she has always finished her flight before he could get in.

## Second Tuesday — He remembers

You bolt the side door. At the village inn you recognize the man in the black-and-white coat. The innkeeper calls him **the Magpie**, because he collects. In the yard stands his carriage, and in it a cabinet full of glass jars. One jar sings with no singer. Its ribbon carries the name of a village girl who, the innkeeper says, lost her beautiful voice one evening and never sang again. When the Magpie whistles her tune, his Haunters answer. “Why borrow a voice when you can keep it?” he says, and shuts the cabinet.

The Magpie smiles at you.

> **The Magpie:** “Keeping a place for Pip, are we?”

He could only have heard that through the side door. Miss Vesper listens when you tell her. She asks what you saw just before her flight ended. The boot is the detail that matters. He doesn't need to remember every old Tuesday. He remembers **yesterday's** and can change what he does next.

You help the children build the **Seeing Spectacles**. Echo hears where an old pair's frames were hidden. Ada retrieves glass from inside a mirror after you help her line up two reflections. Ezra bottles his fog, Silas fits the iron screws, and Miss Vesper gives you a loose feather. Look through the finished glasses and you see hounds against the garden wall, their mouths shaped around empty glass.

You can also inspect the carriage while its owner is at the inn. There are two ways into the cabinet. A spare key hangs from the driver's hook. The cabinet also has a loose iron back panel. A bent catch and the driver's repair chalk show where it can be pried free. Both observations go in your notebook. As the driver tests the loose panel, it catches a jar's clasp and pulls it open. A bright thread escapes toward the village. Inside the inn the girl on the ribbon begins to sing. So that's how it works: open a jar, and the gift flies home. That evening, through the spectacles, you see what guards the carriage after dark: a ring of Haunters, sniffing for anyone who comes near.

At dusk you follow Miss Vesper again, this time wearing the glasses. The Magpie sends his Haunters around the wall before he approaches the front gate. One waits on the roof where he saw her land yesterday. You open the far hatch so she has another place to land. As the front door gives way, the Magpie reaches the hall again. Miss Vesper lands in time to rewind the day before he touches a child. At breakfast, Clem asks you to draw both places where Miss Vesper can land. She can see why neither will be safe the next time.

## Last Tuesday — Protect the house

Miss Vesper can still fly, but the Magpie now knows where to send his hounds to stop her. Another reset would give him another chance to learn. Clem offers to hold the front door. Echo can tell which wall a Haunter is climbing, Ada can take a child through a mirror to safety, and Silas can pull iron across a gap. Miss Vesper lets the children decide what they want to risk.

They choose to stay and protect the house, and tonight Miss Vesper won't fly. Nobody will rewind. And the gift the Magpie wants most of all is Miss Vesper's own: with the power to rewind, he could take anything, again and again. Whatever else happens, his Haunters must not reach her. You tell them what happened when the driver opened a jar. If you can get the cabinet open, the Magpie loses the gifts he uses against you.

Before the dinner bell you can explore, finish helping the children, make a snare or iron barrier and assign defenses. If you have the key and earned Pip's trust, you can send him to the carriage. If you missed either, you can prepare a pry bar and work with Silas to pull the loose panel free from a covered spot. The board shows which approaches are covered and which cabinet plan is ready. You can ring the bell early and lose.

## The final night

You follow Miss Vesper up to the roof one last time. She takes the path she always takes, then stops with one hand on the hatch. Below, Clem gives the signal that the children are in place. Miss Vesper stays where she is. For the first time the children see the stars.

Then the fog comes.

Ezra shows the garden Haunters in his fog so you can close the snare or hold them behind Silas's iron barrier. Clem wakes the front door when the Magpie knocks. Inside, Echo hears claws in the wall and Ada can pull a child through a mirror before a hound reaches them. You use the spectacles to see exactly where each defense needs to go. The scene holds long enough to choose.

When the Magpie enters the yard wearing the gifts he's taken, you draw him away from his carriage. If Pip is ready, he holds his breath, walks straight past the Haunters guarding the carriage, and opens the cabinet with the key. Otherwise, Silas holds the iron back panel steady while you use the prepared pry bar on the damaged catch. Either way you open the jars and the stolen gifts fly home. One song comes out so loudly the Magpie drops his hat.

Without the gifts, he can't command the Haunters. They follow the empty scent back to the carriage and scatter into the fog. The Magpie is left standing in the yard, an ordinary man in a black-and-white coat.

If the Haunters reach Miss Vesper or you leave too many approaches uncovered, the night is lost. **Try Again** returns you to the last Tuesday, before you rang the bell, with everything you had. You can go back for the missed clue or make a different plan.

## Wednesday

At sunrise Miss Vesper asks the children if they want her to fly again. Pip wants to know if anyone will remember where he hung his coat. Clem says she will. They decide to leave the clock alone. Down at the quay, the morning ferry brings a small parcel: the new hinge, due Wednesday, delivered at last. Clem laughs until she cries.

You sign the guestbook again. On Tuesday the signature vanished each time. This one stays. The children head for the village. The ones you helped get a small moment that follows from what you did. Ada watches her reflection in a puddle and sees it wave back. Ezra opens his mouth to announce an encore and decides he'd rather listen to the birds.

The cliff door is still standing in the grass. Miss Vesper walks you to it, and you go home. Back in the present, the old guestbook is open in the ferry shelter. Your signature is there beside six little marks the children drew while the ink was drying. The date is **Wednesday, 18 October 1899**.

## Three loop rules

1. **Miss Vesper rewinds Tuesday.** When the nightjar completes her circle, the coast returns to that morning. The present-day ferry shelter is outside the repeat.
2. **Miss Vesper and anyone inside Vesper House when the circle closes remember.** Most islanders and the Haunters forget. The Magpie remembers after stepping over the sill.
3. **The visitor keeps their memories and what they carry.** Whatever you carry stays with you, and everything else goes back where it was.

## Names that earn their place

| Name | How the player learns it |
|---|---|
| **Morrow Isle** | It's on the ferry sign. *Morrow* is an old word for *tomorrow*, the one thing the children never get to see, until the end. |
| **Vesper House** and **Miss Vesper** | The sign names the house after its headmistress. Her evening flight gives the name its meaning. |
| **The Magpie** | The innkeeper calls him that when you recognize his black-and-white coat and see the things he collects. |
| **Haunters** | The children already have a name for the things they hear at the windows after dusk. |
| **Clem Rouse, Echo Hart, Ezra Fogg, Silas Ironside, Ada Glass and Pip Little** | The player meets them through the dumbwaiter, remembered sounds, the fog show, flying iron, a mirror and a skipped headcount. Use first names in dialogue. |

They're the owner's favorite part of classic adventure games. Keep the house, village, cliff, inn and carriage as the core locations. The player explores, talks to everyone, collects and combines items, makes the glasses, and prepares before committing to the siege. Before implementation, give each required step two in-world clues and test both cabinet routes.

| Required beat | Two starting clues |
|---|---|
| Find the door and follow the first flight | The notebook drawing and wear on the sill teach the knock. Painted wings and a child pointing toward the roof teach where to follow. |
| Make and use the spectacles | Echo hears the repair and the frames leave a mark where they were hidden. A lens drawing and a classroom demonstration show how the fog and feather work together. |
| Discover why the Magpie remembers | The player sees his boot across the sill during the rewind. The next morning he repeats Clem's words about Pip. |

| Prepare and recover | Each approach shows a trace in the spectacles and a physical sign such as a bent bolt. The board names missing parts and a retry returns the player to preparation with the required supplies available. |
