## Your idea, played back to you

You read letters tucked into a book and follow them to Morrow Isle. There, you find a door to a Tuesday that has been repeating since 1899. Miss Fenwick turns into a nightjar at dusk and flies the boundary to wind the day back. The eight children in her care have learned the safest way to live inside that day. Then a stranger walks through the door with a tomorrow the monsters can smell.

The player explores a house with a tide-operated pantry lift and a corridor that seems to go out of its way to avoid a particular child. You learn the islanders' routines, win over the people who live there, and make things from what they lend you. You build the glasses that let you see what's coming. What you learn and build decides whether the household reaches Wednesday together.

## The pitch

At the present-day ferry shelter, you borrow a battered copy of *A Pocket Guide to Morrow Tides*. Inside its back cover, behind a split in the lining, is a sealed packet addressed to the name you gave the ticket clerk. The sheets carry an 1899 tide-office stamp. The last says:

> *Take the book with you. At slack water, knock twice on the upright and once on the sill. If a tall man offers you a receipt, read what it's for before you sign.*

Below it, in a cramped postscript:

> *Leave time to make the glasses.*

On the island, you find a doorframe standing by itself on the cliff. Two knuckles on wood, one heel on stone. *Tap, tap, boom.* A house appears around the door. A girl on a ladder peers past you.

> **Clem:** “You're not the replacement hinge.”  
> **Narrator:** For years to come, this would remain the warmest greeting you had received from a ladder.

The last parcel Clem was promised was a hinge due on the original Tuesday. She's kept trying the door's parcel bell ever since. When you touch the welcome plate, its automatic chalk register traces your ferry stamp over the house's closed date. In the cliffs, something that has never found this Tuesday before stops to listen.

### Why they stay and what the Tallyman wants

Once the plate has stamped the visitor's date, it drops the outer latch. Turning the handle from inside visibly opens onto the same entry hall. The next safe exit needs an open shutter. Even before the latch falls, the player can refuse Fenwick's offer to join the roll and explore the hall, then return to accept. Signing in is a promise to help, not the event that seals the house. The visitor has seen a child recoil from a draft with something's fingerprints in it. On the next Tuesday, they know the children are trying to make a choice their headmistress has postponed for a century.

Fenwick built the shelter during a storm when the house's heat, roof and rescue boat were gone. The compact let her repeat the day while she kept the shutter closed. The island around her rewinds with it. The Tallyman administers the other half of the compact. Once a time scent gives him the shelter's location, he can set his bailiffs against the shutter and file a claim on the first unguarded night.

He wants eight *claims*, one future from every child originally named on the ward. Fenwick holds the ward and can close it, but her own name is on the keeper line. The outsider witness is beyond his claim, though the Haunters can still drag the visitor out of an unwarded scene. At each breach a bailiff can seize one child and file their entry in a custody folio outside the shutter. Until the bailiff crosses, the child can be pulled clear. After that, a talking receipt is all you have. A finger tapping from inside a page is as frightening as it gets. At first light, an entry in the custody folio becomes permanent. The compact supplies two ways to end the active writ and prevent further claims. The Tallyman knows both and will do what he can to keep the player away from the necessary fittings.

## What you do

Read a room, talk to the people in it, and try tools on things with a reason. Inspection adds observations to a notebook. It remembers where a clue was found, and Wren's first hint points the player back to it. Her second hint connects the clue to the relevant tool or person. It works through the notebook once she teaches you how to listen. Day Three's board uses the same observations to show the preparations you know how to make, their destinations and anything you still need.

Typical loop: learn that the keeper rings his break bell early whenever the bakery offers a hot roll, confirm this on his greasy break chart, trade for a roll, use it to move his break, then ask Silas to fish out the key while the keeper's inspection walkway is clear. The same player who is stuck on the keeper can walk back to the house and make progress on the frames, fog, or the twins.

The **Seeing Spectacles** take the menace from traces to bodies. Without them, you can see an indented cushion or the wake through Ezra's fog. With them, the Haunters look like figures made of slack tide lines and borrowed silhouettes. Their seams reveal what each is following. A dark hook on a bell cord tells you more about the next attack than a combat stat.

## The main quest — the Seeing Spectacles

The old house frames hold two spaced lighthouse blanks. Ezra's fog turns a creature's movement into a wake. Fenwick's feather fixes that wake in the air gap. The beacon establishes a fixed reference so each eye sees the same outline. The player has seen each property independently by the time they assemble the glasses. They can disassemble at the bench and recover all registered parts. No required part is spent in the siege.

The spectacles can toggle freely. Off, they leave the painted scenes clear. On, they reveal tracks, seams and short annotations added to the notebook. Mark shapes and motion as well as color. Subtitles and icons convey every relevant tick, chime and clue. There is no dexterity gate on fitting the lenses or operating a trap.

## Where's the classic adventure feel?

The lift, the cave and the lenses are discoveries you earn with observation and inventory combinations. A few good jokes come from treating the world as a collection of toys. Offer a real roll to the wrong lighthouse keeper window and he lowers a basket that already contains a cutlery set. Try an ordinary button in the tick lure and Ezra holds it up to his ear. “A very quiet instrument. We shall book it for the interval.” At the end, the player reads the Tallyman's weapon as carefully as they read the book that started the game.
