## Your idea, played back to you

You find a letter tucked inside an old book and follow it to Morrow Isle. There, a door with nothing behind it opens onto a Tuesday that has been repeating since 1899. Every dusk, Miss Fenwick turns into a nightjar and flies once around the house, and the day winds back to morning. Eight unusual children have learned to live safely inside that one day. Then you walk in carrying a tomorrow, and something that has hunted them for 127 years can finally smell where they are.

You explore the house and the island, learn everyone's routines, and win over the people who live there. You build the glasses that let you see what's coming, and you prepare for the one night the house has never had. What you learned, built and promised decides whether everyone reaches Wednesday.

## The pitch

At the present-day ferry shelter there's a shelf of left-behind books. You borrow a battered *Pocket Guide to Morrow Tides*. Behind a split in its back lining is a sealed envelope on 1899 tide-office paper, addressed to you by the name you just gave the ticket clerk. It contains one letter:

> *Take the book. At slack water, knock twice on the door that isn't there and stamp once on the sill. Leave time to make the glasses. And if a tall man shows you a receipt, count the names.*
> *— [your initial]*

On the island cliff stands a doorframe with nothing behind it. Two knuckles on the wood, one heel on the stone: *tap, tap, boom.* A house unfolds around the door: a warm hall, too many coats, and a girl up a ladder who peers past you.

> **Clem:** "You're not the replacement hinge."
> **Narrator:** For years to come, this would remain the warmest greeting you had ever received from a ladder.

It's Tuesday, the 17th of October, 1899, and it has been that Tuesday for 127 years. The household has been waiting all that time for a hinge that was due to arrive today. When you touch the brass welcome plate, it copies the date stamp off your ferry ticket: a date from the future, on a house that has no future. Far out in the cliffs, something that has spent 127 years looking for this house lifts its head.

## The rules of the loop

There are six rules. Players learn each one by seeing it happen, not by being told.

1. **Tuesday repeats.** Every dusk, Fenwick becomes a nightjar and flies once around the house. When she lands, the great tide clock in the cellar chimes, and everything goes back to 7 a.m. on Tuesday. The weather, the tide and the villagers all repeat. The present-day island outside the door keeps ordinary time.
2. **The household remembers.** Fenwick, the children and anyone signed on the house roll remember everything. The villagers don't.
3. **Your satchel is outside Tuesday.** Whatever you carry at dusk stays with you, and the place you took it from stays empty. Anything you set down goes home at the next reset. Repairs reset. Promises and friendships don't. *(The exact item rules are in the Designer's notes.)*
4. **Small turns are free.** Before dusk, the household can turn the hall clock to a discovered window: *morning*, *low tide* or *late afternoon*. This changes village appointments, tide and lighting while you explore. It preserves what you've taken, doors you've opened and the choices you've made that Tuesday. You never wait around or miss a window. Fenwick's dusk flight is what resets the day.
5. **The shutter has three teeth.** In the tide clock, a great shutter holds the night out. Under pressure, the dusk flight breaks a tooth: one on Day One, another on Day Two. The third tooth is the latch. A third flight would break it, so Fenwick keeps it shut until the player rings for the night. The shutter and welcome plate sit on the boundary, so the broken teeth and your ferry stamp survive the reset.
6. **The household can leave only into Wednesday.** Anyone who lived through the first Tuesday can't step out of the door into another year. The door lets them out only into the day after. That's why the children can't simply follow you home.

**Two special devices, each taught once:**

- **The Letterbox** *(taught on Day Two)*. The old front-door letterbox posts paper to **the receiving slot and the morning printed on its stamp**. The house stamp shows a plate icon and sends a note to the breakfast plate. The ferry ticket shows a book icon and sends it to the ferry shelter's guide pocket. Objects and people can't travel this way.
- **The Rehearsal** *(taught on Day Three)*. Fenwick sets the tide clock's small hand to REHEARSE. If the night goes wrong, everyone wakes on the last Tuesday morning. Captured children are home again. You keep your satchel and your notes, the children keep their promises, and the defenses need setting up again, though the board remembers your plan. It works for that one night as many times as you need, until you accept Wednesday. It never mends a shutter tooth. *(Exact rules in the Designer's notes.)*

## Why the danger is real

In 1899, **the Tallyman** came to Morrow Isle. He collects tomorrows for the Office of Days. Uncommon children have uncommon tomorrows, and the Office values those very highly. He had a writ for all of Fenwick's children, to be served on Tuesday night.

Fenwick used the oldest protection a keeper of unusual children has: **the shelter compact**. A writ can only be served at night, and a sheltered day has no night. She folded Tuesday over itself before he could knock. For 127 years he has waited outside time, unable to tell which Tuesday they were hiding in.

Then the welcome plate copied your ferry stamp, a date from the future, onto a house that had none. To the Tallyman it was like a lit window on a dark coast. The latch drops when the plate stamps the date. From inside, the door opens onto the hall again until the shutter is raised. Signing the visitor's line is your choice to help the people in the house. It is the stamp that has brought the bailiffs to their door.

**His rules:**

- While the shutter holds, he can't come in and can't take anyone.
- By day, his **Haunters** (his bailiffs) lean on the house's cracks. You'll see frost on a window, a draft with fingerprints in it, a cushion that dents when no one is sitting on it. Out on the island, where the house's protection is thin, they can drag an outsider out of Tuesday altogether. (That's a Try Again.)
- On the night the shutter opens, his writ can be served. A Haunter that catches a child flattens them into a claim slip. The slip counts as paper at the sill. Until the bailiff carries it across to the outside folio, you can pull the child back.
- A writ can be served only at night and must describe the household that lived here on the first Tuesday. The printed writ is fixed for this night. If a witness reads the correct roll into the record before first light, a mismatched writ and every claim under it become void. Without that challenge, a child already filed beyond the sill stays there, even if the Ballast Trap sends the Tallyman away and stops him from making further claims.

## What you do

Read a room, talk to the people in it, and try things on things for a reason. When you inspect something, a note goes into your notebook, along with where you found the clue. Once Wren learns to listen, she becomes your hint-giver. Her first hint points you back to a clue you've already found, and her second connects that clue to the right tool or person. On Day Three, the same notebook feeds the preparation board, which shows what you know how to build, where it goes, and what you're still missing.

**A typical puzzle loop:** you learn that Keeper Moss takes his break early whenever the bakery offers a hot roll, and you confirm it on his greasy break chart. You trade for a roll, use it to move his break, and then ask Silas to fish out the key while the keeper's walkway is clear. If you're stuck on the keeper, you can walk back to the house and make progress on the frames, the fog or the twins instead.

The **Seeing Spectacles** turn the menace from traces into bodies. Without them you see a dented cushion, or a wake through Ezra's fog. With them, the Haunters look like figures stitched from slack tide-lines and borrowed silhouettes. Their seams show what each one is following. A dark hook on a bell cord tells you more about the next attack than any combat stat.

### The Tallyman and the Haunters

The Tallyman wears his hat in the rain so the water never reaches the receipts. He hands over a slip even when a Haunter takes a teaspoon. ## The structure: three Tuesdays and a night

Each numbered thread is a puzzle, with its outcome and two in-world clues. Several threads can be worked on at once, so being stuck on one never means being stuck on everything.

### Prologue: The Door That Isn't There

At the ferry shelter, the guide's back cover is oddly thick. A torn stitch and a fleck of red sealing wax are two signs that something is inside. Lift the lining with the edge of your ticket stub. The ferry shelter doubles as the island's lending library (*Take a book for the crossing*), so its stamp on your ticket stub shows a little book. The same book icon marks a notch inside the pocket, a detail that will mean more later. On the cliff, use the tide page to read the slack-water marker. The letter gives you the knock, and two worn dimples in the doorframe and a boot-scuff on the sill show you where. The empty frame fills with a door.

*Teaches:* looking, taking, using and combining items.

### The Siege: Close the Shutter

The dinner bell calls the children in. Fenwick opens the worn shutter on her own terms, giving the household a chance to stand together. Night falls on the house for the first time in 127 years. Ezra starts a stage whisper about the stars and forgets to finish it. Then the Tallyman knocks. He has a stamp for the keyhole.

The siege is four scenes. At each one, the spectacles let you read the attack before you act, and using what you prepared is the puzzle. The scene waits for your choice. A wrong tool can be taken back and tried somewhere else. Choosing to pass or confirming an ineffective response after its warning counts as a breach.

| Stage | How to win it | If it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| **Front step** | Spring the snare when the spectacles show the lead bailiff inside the loop. *Or:* Ezra lays a fog wake and Agnes lets a true tick out of her button to draw the whole group across it into the closing door. | Ezra is cut off on the step. |
| **Upper landing** | Clem drops the loaded lift across the shaft. *Or:* Ada's crossing gets Agnes past the bailiffs into the nursery. Agnes shuts the landing's far door from safety while you close the near one. | Agnes is cut off at the landing. |
| **Cellar** | Silas calls the bolt down its rail, *or* you release the drop-bar as the lead hook passes the stop. | Hettie is cut off by the clock. |
| **Roof** *(just before first light)* | **The Small Print:** show the seven-child writ and Fenwick's attested roll at the witness stand, then read the true roll. All claims under the void writ fall away. **Or the Ballast Trap:** offer the clock as payment and drop the shutter after the Tallyman steps on the plate. | Without a ready route you can still read the room, but you can't build a missing part on the roof. **Breaches and rescues.** Two failed approaches break the ward before you reach the roof. After one failure, the exposed child is dragged toward the sill. You can spend the rescue you've prepared: Hettie's hook works from the safe anchor at any approach, and Ada's unused crossing works at its chosen mirror pair. The scene waits for you to act or pass. Once the child crosses the sill, only the Small Print can recall the filed claim during that attempt.

**Planning conflicts.** If you've assigned the same ally or item to two jobs that can't both happen, the board tells you before you ring the bell. An ally who closes a passage can still move on to the roof afterwards.

**The Small Print, in play.** Just before first light on the roof, the Tallyman presents the writ. Show the attested roll alongside the rubbing or Pip's copy. Select "being seven" and the eighth name. The game asks you whether to read the true roll into the record. When you choose to do it, the Tallyman's lips move with yours, counting. At Pip he stops. Pip takes a breath and keeps breathing. "Eight," he says. The writ curls like paper near a fire. The Tallyman closes his ledger. His quill snaps, which offends him more than losing. A child filed under the void writ steps back through the sill before daylight.

**The Ballast Trap, in play.** Click the prepared clock or let Agnes release the tick by the working clock on the plate. Offer it as payment. The Tallyman steps forward to inspect it and clips its tag to his ledger. The spectacles show that the link is complete. On the plate, the old arrow points through the sill. Pull the shutter with Clem while the jaws or pawl hold. The plate carries the Tallyman and his attached writ out of Tuesday and stamps **RETURNED BEFORE DAWN**. He can take no further claims here. A child already in the outside folio remains there.

### Endings

- **Everyone reaches Wednesday.** You win on the roof with no child left in the folio, or with the Small Print, which brings everyone home. On the cellar stairs, Fenwick asks the children whether to let the clock go. One wants to see a snow day. One wants to know whether yesterday's dough ever rises. Pip takes a breath before the first count that starts with his name. The children unlock the front door themselves.
- **A quiet Wednesday.** You win with the Ballast Trap while one child is still filed in the folio. Winning closes the writ, but it can't recall a claim that has already crossed the sill. That child speaks through a receipt until daylight, and then the line goes quiet. The others keep their place at breakfast. **Before you accept this ending, the game offers you the Rehearsal.**
- **The house falls.** Two breaches, or a roof with no route ready. As the Tallyman writes, Fenwick slips the small hand loose. The Rehearsal restores the last Tuesday morning. The board marks the first failed approach or the missing roof prerequisite and points to its sources. You can reclaim spent parts, reapply earlier assignments, or explore and try a different route.

Every ending tells you which branch you reached and what happened to everyone in it.

### Epilogue: Dear ___

The children take one ordinary Wednesday step onto the 1899 quay. Midge the ferryhand comes up the gangplank with a small parcel: "Your hinge, miss. Told you it'd come Wednesday." Fenwick introduces the children to a fisherman who has never seen them before. The door back to the present is open just long enough for you to leave. You say goodbye. The Wednesday they step into is the long-ago past of the present you came from.

At the desk in the open doorway, Fenwick brings the house roll to say goodbye. If you skipped Pip's thread, she asks him to sign his name now. Even after a Ballast victory you see what a difference one name makes. You take the guide from your satchel and match the paper you found to the tide-office stock on the desk. With the original note as a reference in your notebook, you write the same instructions: take the book, the knock, the glasses and *count the names*. You sign with your initial. The handwriting matches, down to where the pen catches on the fold.

Then you stamp the envelope with **your own ferry ticket**, with its book icon and its earlier present-day morning. The icon matches the one inside the guide's pocket. The Letterbox sends the paper to that receiving slot. If you try a different version or stamp, the box returns the envelope and the notebook stays open for a correction. The next shot is that earlier dawn at the ferry shelter. The same guide sits on the shelf. Its back lining thickens, and a fleck of red wax catches the edge of the crack.

Back on the present-day quay, someone carved eight sets of initials into the old weather board, long ago. P.M. is last, and it's carved twice as deep as the rest. On the back is a row of tally marks, one for every Wednesday they lived.

*(In the quiet ending, there are seven sets of initials and a space where the eighth should be.)*

## The main quest: the Seeing Spectacles

The old house frames hold two spaced lighthouse lens blanks. Ezra's fog turns a creature's movement into a wake, Fenwick's feather fixes that wake in the gap between the lenses, and the beacon gives a fixed reference so both eyes see the same outline. By the time you assemble the spectacles, you have seen each of these properties separately. You can take them apart at the workbench and get every part back, and no required part is used up in the siege.

You can put the spectacles on and take them off freely. Off, the painted scenes stay clear. On, they show tracks, seams and short notes added to your notebook. They use shape and motion as well as color, and subtitles and icons convey every important tick, chime and clue. No puzzle needs fast fingers.

## Where's the classic adventure feel?

The lift, the cave and the lenses are discoveries you earn by looking closely and combining things. Some of the best jokes come from treating the world as a box of toys. Offer a hot roll at the wrong lighthouse window and the keeper lowers a basket that already holds a cutlery set. Try an ordinary button as the tick lure and Ezra holds it to his ear: "A very quiet instrument. We shall book it for the interval." In the end you defeat the Tallyman the way you started the game, by reading carefully.

## Scope

The game has **23 locations**. Rooms you already know get new props, routes and lighting as the Tuesdays change, and the siege reuses the front step, upper landing, cellar and roof.

| Area | Locations |
|---|---|

| **House (13)** | Front step, entry hall, kitchen, pantry lift, library, nursery, Fenwick's study, laundry, upper landing, attic workbench, garden and greenhouse, cellar tide clock, roof walk |

## The rhyme

The children clap it in the hall while Fenwick flies at dusk: two hand-taps, then a foot-stamp on *boom*, just like the knock on the door that isn't there.

> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Tuesday's back in every room.*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom bay,*
> *Haunters never find the way.*
> *Wind it back and wind it tight,*
> *Never, never comes the night.*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Good morning, Morrow! Make some room!*

They change the last line only after you get them through the night:

> *Here comes Wednesday! Make some room!*

## Designer's notes

The exact rules behind the plain-language versions above, for building the game. Players never need to read these.

- **Satchel accounting.** When an item goes into a carried assembly, the assembly holds its place in the satchel's record. A spent or traded part comes back at its source once no carried result is reserving it. A part available in one place is absent everywhere else.
- **Rehearsal snapshot.** It restores the last Tuesday morning and clears every siege capture. The satchel keeps checked-out items and their carried assemblies. Fittings and placed defenses reset. The board can reapply earlier assignments and recover spent materials, skipping any part still in the satchel or in an intact assembly. It repeats for the same night until Wednesday is accepted, and never replaces a shutter tooth.
- **Lure parts.** The snare can use the boathouse's wind-up movement directly, or a tick stored in Agnes's button. The ballast plate has its own winding socket and uses a separate clock-room movement if Agnes's button is already committed. Finished preparations can be recovered from the board before the bell.
- **The year count.** The game reads today's date from the player's computer and computes the years since 17 October 1899 (127 as of November 2026). Every on-screen "127 years" uses that number.
