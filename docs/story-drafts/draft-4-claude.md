## Your idea, played back to you

You find letters tucked inside an old book and follow them to Morrow Isle. There, a door with nothing behind it opens onto a Tuesday that has been repeating since 1899. Every dusk, Miss Fenwick turns into a nightjar and flies once around the house, and the day winds back to morning. Eight unusual children have learned to live safely inside that one day. Then you walk in carrying a tomorrow, and something that has hunted them for 126 years can finally smell where they are.

You explore the house and the island, learn everyone's routines, and win over the people who live there. You build the glasses that let you see what's coming, and you prepare for the one night the house has never had. What you learned, built and promised decides whether everyone reaches Wednesday.

## The pitch

At the present-day ferry shelter there's a shelf of left-behind books. You borrow a battered *Pocket Guide to Morrow Tides*. Behind a split in its back lining is a sealed packet of letters on 1899 tide-office paper, addressed to you by the name you just gave the ticket clerk. The last letter says:

> *Take the book. At slack water, knock twice on the door that isn't there and stamp once on the sill. Leave time to make the glasses. And if a tall man shows you a receipt, count the names.*
> *— [your initial]*

On the island cliff stands a doorframe with nothing behind it. Two knuckles on the wood, one heel on the stone: *tap, tap, boom.* A house unfolds around the door: a warm hall, too many coats, and a girl up a ladder who peers past you.

> **Clem:** "You're not the replacement hinge."
> **Narrator:** For years to come, this would remain the warmest greeting you had ever received from a ladder.

It's Tuesday, the 17th of October, 1899, and it has been that Tuesday for 126 years. The household has been waiting all that time for a hinge that was due to arrive today. When you touch the brass welcome plate, it copies the date stamp off your ferry ticket: a date from the future, on a house that has no future. Far out in the cliffs, something that has spent 126 years looking for this house lifts its head.

## The rules of the loop

There are six rules. Players learn each one by seeing it happen, not by being told.

1. **Tuesday repeats.** Every dusk, Fenwick becomes a nightjar and flies once around the house. When she lands, the great tide clock in the cellar chimes, and everything goes back to 7 a.m. on Tuesday. The weather, the tide and the villagers all repeat. The present-day island outside the door keeps ordinary time.
2. **The household remembers.** Fenwick, the children and anyone signed on the house roll remember everything. The villagers don't.
3. **Your satchel is outside Tuesday.** Whatever you're carrying at dusk stays with you, and the place you took it from stays empty, because you've signed it out of Tuesday. Anything you put down goes home at the reset. Repairs reset. Promises and friendships don't.
4. **Small turns are free.** Before dusk, the household can turn the hall clock to any hour of Tuesday: *morning*, *low tide* or *late afternoon*. You never wait around, and you can never miss a window. The full turn at dusk is the costly one.
5. **The shutter has three teeth.** In the tide clock, a great shutter holds the night out. For 126 years nothing pushed on it. Now something is, and every dusk reset under that pressure snaps a tooth: one on Day One, another on Day Two. On Day Three there is nothing left to hold, and the night is coming whether they're ready or not.
6. **The household can leave only into Wednesday.** Anyone who lived through the first Tuesday can't step out of the door into another year. The door lets them out only into the day after. That's why the children can't simply follow you home.

**Two special devices, each taught once:**

- **The Letterbox** *(taught on Day Two)*. The old front-door letterbox posts a letter to **the place and the morning printed on its stamp**. With the house's own stamp, that means this Tuesday morning. It carries only paper, never people or objects.
- **The Rehearsal** *(taught on Day Three)*. On the last Tuesday, Fenwick sets the tide clock's small hand to REHEARSE. If the night goes wrong, she can bring everyone back to that morning, as many times as it takes, and you keep everything you carry and know. It works only for that one night, and it never touches the shutter.

## Why the danger is real

In 1899, **the Tallyman** came to Morrow Isle. He collects tomorrows for the Office of Days. Uncommon children have uncommon tomorrows, and the Office values those very highly. He had a writ for all of Fenwick's children, to be served on Tuesday night.

Fenwick used the oldest protection a keeper of unusual children has: **the shelter compact**. A writ can only be served at night, and a sheltered day has no night. She folded Tuesday over itself before he could knock. For 126 years he has waited outside time, unable to tell which Tuesday they were hiding in.

Then the welcome plate copied your ferry stamp, a date from the future, onto a house with no future. To the Tallyman it was like a lit window on a dark coast.

**His rules:**

- While the shutter holds, he can't come in and can't take anyone.
- By day, his **Haunters** (his bailiffs) lean on the house's cracks. You'll see frost on a window, a draft with fingerprints in it, a cushion that dents when no one is sitting on it. Out on the island, where the house's protection is thin, they can drag an outsider out of Tuesday altogether. (That's a Try Again.)
- On the night the shutter opens, his writ can be served. A Haunter that drags a child across the outer sill files them in the Tallyman's folio. Until it crosses the sill, the child can still be pulled back.
- A writ must be served **before first light**, and it must **describe the household correctly**. He cannot lie on paper, which is why he's so fond of small print.

Wren can tell you where to find any part you're known to be missing. The board explains that a rescue saves a child but doesn't stop a breach.

### The Siege: Close the Shutter

The dinner bell calls the children in. Fenwick opens the worn shutter on her own terms, giving the household a chance to stand together. The sun sets on the house for the first time in 126 years. Ezra starts a stage whisper about the stars and forgets to finish it. Then the Tallyman knocks. He has a stamp for the keyhole.

The siege is four scenes. At each one, the spectacles let you read the attack before you act, and using what you prepared is the puzzle. The scene waits for your choice. A wrong tool can be taken back and tried somewhere else.

| Stage | How to win it | If it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| **Front step** | Spring the snare when the spectacles show the lead bailiff inside the loop. *Or:* Ezra lays a fog wake and Agnes lets a true tick out of her button to draw the whole group across it into the closing door. | Ezra is cut off on the step. |
| **Upper landing** | Clem drops the loaded lift across the shaft. *Or:* Ada's crossing empties the landing, and the bailiffs are shut in behind the glass. | Agnes is cut off at the landing. |
| **Cellar** | Silas calls the bolt down its rail, *or* you release the drop-bar as the lead hook passes the stop. | Hettie is cut off by the clock. |
| **Roof** *(the last minutes before first light)* | **The Small Print:** read the house roll aloud against the writ. Eight names, not seven: the writ is void, and so is every claim made under it. **Or the Ballast Trap:** the Tallyman steps onto the plate after the tick, Clem drops the shutter, and he and his writ go out through the sill to the outside of Tuesday. | If you have no route ready, you can still look around, but you can't build anything new on the roof. The Tallyman wins, and the Rehearsal begins. |

**Breaches and rescues.** If two stages break, the ward fails before you reach the roof, and the Rehearsal begins. If one stage breaks, a bailiff drags that child toward the sill. If you have a rescue ready (Hettie's hook or Ada's unused crossing), you're offered it before the sill. If not, the child is filed in the folio. But if you win by the Small Print, every claim is void, and they come home.

**Planning conflicts.** If you've assigned the same ally or item to two jobs that can't both happen, the board tells you before you ring the bell. An ally who closes a passage can still move on to the roof afterwards.

**The Small Print, in play.** At first light on the roof, the Tallyman presents the writ. You unroll the house roll beside it and read the names aloud, one by one. The Tallyman's lips move with you, counting. When you reach Pip, he stops. Pip takes a breath and *doesn't* hold it. "Eight," he says. The writ curls at the edges like paper near a fire. The Tallyman closes his ledger. His quill snaps, which offends him more than losing.

**The Ballast Trap, in play.** Agnes lets the tick out on the plate. Through the spectacles you watch the seam that links the Tallyman's ledger to the sound. He steps forward, unable to stop himself. Clem pulls the rope. The shutter drops, and the plate takes him and everything attached to him out through the sill. On the far side, it stamps **RETURNED BEFORE DAWN**.

### Endings

- **Everyone reaches Wednesday.** You win on the roof with no child left in the folio, or with the Small Print, which brings everyone home. On the cellar stairs, Fenwick asks the children whether to let the clock go. One wants to see a snow day. One wants to know whether yesterday's dough ever rises. Pip takes a breath before the first count that starts with his name. The children unlock the front door themselves.
- **A quiet Wednesday.** You win with the Ballast Trap while one child is still filed in the folio. Winning closes the writ, but it can't recall a claim that has already crossed the sill. That child speaks through a receipt until daylight, and then the line goes quiet. The others keep their place at breakfast. **Before you accept this ending, the game offers you the Rehearsal.**
- **The house falls.** Two breaches, or a roof with no route ready. As the Tallyman writes, Fenwick slips the small hand loose. Everyone wakes on the last Tuesday morning, keeping everything you carry and know. The board now circles the stage that broke first and points to where its missing parts can be found.

Every ending tells you which branch you reached and what happened to everyone in it.

### Epilogue: Dear ___

The children take one ordinary Wednesday step onto the 1899 quay. Midge the ferryhand comes up the gangplank with a small parcel: "Your hinge, miss. Told you it'd come Wednesday." Fenwick introduces the children to a fisherman who has never seen them before. The door back to the present is open just long enough for you to leave. You say goodbye. The Wednesday they step into is the long-ago past of the present you came from.

At the desk in the open doorway, you take the guide out of your satchel and compare the letters you found with the tide-office paper on the desk. They're the same stock. You copy the knock from the marks you saw on the sill. You add the warning you now know is true: *count the names.* You sign with your initial. The handwriting matches, right down to the place where the pen catches on the fold.

Then you stamp the packet with **your own ferry ticket**, the present-day date the welcome plate copied on your first morning. The letterbox takes it. The next shot is that earlier dawn at the ferry shelter: the same book on the same shelf, its empty back lining thickening, a fleck of red wax just catching the edge of the crack.

Back on the present-day quay, someone carved eight sets of initials into the old weather board, long ago. P.M. is last, and it's carved twice as deep as the rest. On the back is a row of tally marks, one for every Wednesday they lived.

*(In the quiet ending, there are seven sets of initials and a space where the eighth should be.)*

## The main quest: the Seeing Spectacles

The old house frames hold two spaced lighthouse lens blanks. Ezra's fog turns a creature's movement into a wake, Fenwick's feather fixes that wake in the gap between the lenses, and the beacon gives a fixed reference so both eyes see the same outline. By the time you assemble the spectacles, you have seen each of these properties separately. You can take them apart at the workbench and get every part back, and no required part is used up in the siege.

You can put the spectacles on and take them off freely. Off, the painted scenes stay clear. On, they show tracks, seams and short notes added to your notebook. They use shape and motion as well as color, and subtitles and icons convey every important tick, chime and clue. No puzzle needs fast fingers.

## How being prepared works

| Example route | Front step, landing, cellar | Roof | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Small Print, few allies** | Snare with a wind-up lure, Clem's lift, drop-bar | The house roll with Pip's name, a look at the writ (the press-plate rubbing), and you reading aloud | All eight reach Wednesday. Even a missed approach is undone, because a void writ voids every claim. |
| **The household ballast** | Snare, Ada's crossing, Silas's bolt | Clem on the shutter, Hettie's pawl, Agnes's tick | All eight reach Wednesday, with no small-print threads needed. |
| **One missed counter** | Two of the three approaches | Ballast trap | A quiet Wednesday, unless a rescue was ready. (Use the Small Print instead and everyone comes home.) |
| **Rushed bell** | Nothing prepared | Nothing ready | Two breaches, the house falls, and the Rehearsal begins. |

The snare can use the boathouse's wind-up movement directly, or a tick stored in Agnes's button. The ballast plate has its own winding socket, so it can use the clock room's spare movement if Agnes's button is already in use. Every finished preparation is either placed, recoverable from the board, or in your satchel. Nothing you need depends on a one-time appointment, and before the bell, any required puzzle can be redone from its source.

## Where's the classic adventure feel?

The lift, the cave and the lenses are discoveries you earn by looking closely and combining things. Some of the best jokes come from treating the world as a box of toys. Offer a hot roll at the wrong lighthouse window and the keeper lowers a basket that already holds a cutlery set. Try an ordinary button as the tick lure and Ezra holds it to his ear: "A very quiet instrument. We shall book it for the interval." In the end you defeat the Tallyman the way you started the game, by reading carefully.

## Scope

The game has **23 locations**. Rooms you already know get new props, routes and lighting as the Tuesdays change, and the siege reuses the front step, upper landing, cellar and roof.

| Area | Locations |
|---|---|
| **Present day (3)** | Ferry shelter and quay, cliff doorframe, threshold desk in the open door |
| **House (13)** | Front step, entry hall, kitchen, pantry lift, library, nursery, Fenwick's study, laundry, upper landing, attic workbench, garden and greenhouse, cellar tide clock, roof walk |
| **Island (7)** | Village square, bakery, tide office, lighthouse upper room, lighthouse lower stair, sea cave, breakwater and boathouse |

Close-ups of the notebook, the preparation board and the workbench are overlays on their rooms. The Wednesday quay reuses the ferry composition. The biggest visual moments are Fenwick's flights, the first look through the spectacles, the twins' hug in the hallway of mirrors, and the stars coming out. All of them use scenery that's already in the game.

## The rhyme

The children clap it in the hall while Fenwick flies at dusk: two hand-taps, then a foot-stamp on *boom*, just like the knock on the door that isn't there.

> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Tuesday's back in every room.*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom bay,*
> *Haunters never find the way.*
> *Wind it back and wind it tight,*
> *Never, never comes the night.*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Good morning, Morrow! Make some room!*

They change the last line only after you get them through the night:

> *Here comes Wednesday! Make some room!*
