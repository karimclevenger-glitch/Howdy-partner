## The story in one breath

An old notebook brings a visitor to Morrow Isle. Behind a door on the cliff, eight children are living the same Tuesday. At dusk the visitor follows their headmistress onto the roof and sees her turn into a nightjar and fly a circuit around the house. A creature reaches through the nursery window. Before it can grab the child inside, Fenwick lands and the day rolls back to breakfast. The child is safe. The scratch the creature left on the boundary is still there.

The visitor helps the children work out what got through. They build glasses that show invisible Haunters and discover who sent them: the Glassmaker, a former lighthouse repairer who can catch a child's gift in glass. He wants the others. The scratches show him how to find the house sooner on each repeat. With one safe flight left and the hidden door at risk, the visitor and the children must work out where he'll come through and prepare the house to stop him.

## Prologue — The door on the cliff

In the present-day ferry shelter, you find a notebook behind a loose shelf board. Its cover says **Morrow House, 1899**. The first pages are ordinary. Someone has written **Clem put the laundry lift in the pantry again** and crossed out **again**. Near the end, a sketch shows a door standing alone on the cliff. Beside it, in the same handwriting, is a note.

> When the tide goes still, two taps on the wood. One stamp on the stone. Please knock. The children get frightened when someone tries the handle.

On the cliff you find that frame, with grass where the house should be. You follow the sketch. Two taps, one stamp. The door opens onto a warm hall and a girl on a ladder.

> **Clem:** “If you're the person who was meant to bring a hinge, you're late.”  
> **Narrator:** Under the circumstances, this was the correct thing to say to almost anyone.

A gust pushes the door back before you can latch it. Three wet scratches appear at the edge of the sill. You see what they do before you know what made them. They run through a chalk line across the door, and the hall falls dark for a breath. The latch catches on the damaged frame. You can stand by the door and try it again. It opens onto the hall on both sides. Fenwick tells you she'll help you go home when she can open it safely.

The notebook was Fenwick's. Before her first reset she left it for a trusted boatman at the ordinary ferry quay, beyond the stretch of coast she planned to rewind. Its pages reached the present the long way. The player can later match the handwriting to her house notebook. The visitor came because a real person left a trace.

## First Tuesday — Follow the headmistress

The children let you explore the house while Fenwick checks the scratch. You get to know Clem through the lift that never goes where it is told, and Ezra through a magic show that consists mainly of a sheet and an argument over applause. If you help Pip put a peg in the hall, his coat gets its own place for the first time.

Near dusk, a cup breaks in the kitchen. Fenwick looks out the window and abruptly leaves her tea. The children start their familiar two taps and a foot stamp. You follow her up the stairs. The roof door has been blown shut, but the children have drawn two white wings by the handle of a little service hatch. Clem tells you Fenwick always takes the way with the painted wings. Open it and you arrive just in time to see her coat fall and a nightjar lift out of it.

Follow her path from the roof to the garden boundary. A Haunter has snagged the broken door line. Its claws leave three tracks in frost as it feels for a way through. You use the lift's spare hook to pull the loose boundary cord back onto its post. A worn groove shows where the cord sits, and a chalk picture beside the post shows how a hook reaches it from behind the line. The creature slips off the cord and rushes the nursery window instead.

Through the window you see Ezra back away. The dent in the curtain is coming closer. Fenwick lands. The cup lifts off the floor in pieces and joins itself on the table. The candle unburns. Ezra is at breakfast, halfway through the same sentence he was saying when you arrived.

You can walk straight back to the garden and look at the post. The cord is in place. **The claw scratch is still there.** Fenwick shows you where the line is laid into the boundary. The marked circuit around the house anchors the repeat that covers this stretch of coast. Things inside the repeat rewind. The marks on its anchors are still waiting when the day starts again.

> **You:** “How many times have you done that?”  
> **Fenwick:** “Enough that I know which cup to catch. I don't usually have to catch a child.”

Only now does she tell you why she keeps the house in Tuesday. In the original 1899 afternoon a man came to ask for the children. Fenwick discovered what he was doing at the lighthouse, shut the door, and rewound before he could reach it. Each time, he and his creatures start the day outside the hidden refuge and forget their approach. Each morning the lamp can feel the scratches and tug its Haunters toward them. It finds the house faster as the scratches spread. The Glassmaker can follow that pull without remembering yesterday.

**The player's next goal:** find what made the scratch and learn what it wants before Fenwick has to fly again.

## Second Tuesday — See what the Haunters are carrying

The scratch shines earlier in the morning than it did yesterday. The children call the invisible things Haunters. At lunch one brushes the kitchen window. Ezra breathes fog across the pane and it parts around a hand. He stops in the middle of asking for an encore. Agnes sets a second bowl facing the mirror, and its reflection lifts a spoon. Fenwick tells everyone what the scratch means. Clem asks to see the damage for herself.

From that point, three investigations are open.

- **The lighthouse at low tide.** Keeper Moss guards the lower stair until his break. Trade the baker her tray hook for a hot roll, then bring it to the keeper. Silas can pull the lost iron gate key up its guide rail once you show him its shape. In the sea cave, drain the flooded workbench and find two rejected lenses and a sign under the grime. **Glass repairs. Lighthouse lenses.** The mark on the glass is the same one Fenwick remembers from the old keeper's lantern.
- **The rooms inside the house.** Make Wren a cork-and-felt pad so she can hear which library spindle was removed. Behind it are empty spectacle frames. Upstairs, line up two mirrors with Clem's help. Agnes has been carrying Ada's laugh in a button. Let her decide to open it. Ada follows the sound, and the twins finally meet in the space between the reflections. Ada fetches the old lens gauge from her side of the glass.
- **Ezra's fog and Fenwick's feather.** Make a bottle with a gas-trap stopper and ask Ezra to breathe through the correct port. Fenwick's old classroom exercise shows how her feathers make tide marks hold in fog. She gives you a loose one. At the bench, fit the lenses, feed in the fog and draw the feather across the inlet. Use the lighthouse's fixed marks to line up the view.

At the breakwater, the glasses show a Haunter for the first time. Its fingers end in fine glass threads. Through the glasses you can follow one thread from the broken line to a shuttered workshop in the lighthouse. You see a man step outside and tap a little glass cell in his lamp. Fog pours out. When it clears, his outline is gone in the ordinary view, but the glasses show glass threads woven into his coat. The maker's mark from the workshop door is stamped on his lamp. Fenwick later calls him the Glassmaker. Ezra is standing beside you, trying to make the same trick work. He can manage only a puff. The thing at the kitchen window took a little of his gift.

In the workshop you find the cracked cell he discarded. Set it on the test stand and turn it to face Ezra. A wisp of fog runs back to his hand. When he tries again, he makes a full cloud. An old drawing shows the same two directions, a hand into the lamp on one side and the lamp back to a hand on the other. Fenwick recognizes the device. Years ago the Glassmaker borrowed a little of Ezra's fog to guide a ferry away from a reef. When Ezra asked for the rest back, the Glassmaker kept the cell. He thinks every useful gift belongs in his hands. Now he has made Haunters to fetch the others.

> **Ezra:** “I said he could borrow it. I meant he'd have to ask again.”

At dusk, you follow Fenwick a second time. This time you wear the glasses. The visitor sees two threads tug on the circuit from opposite ends. You help Fenwick free one, but the other cuts across the repaired line. She completes the flight with a torn wing. Tuesday begins again. Fenwick's wing is whole, but the outer line is down at the front step, the landing and the cellar. Another circuit would take her past three open gaps with the Haunters inside reach.

**The player's next goal:** learn how to stop the lamp and keep its owner from following the children into the hidden refuge.

## Last Tuesday — Prepare the house

The Glassmaker stands at the garden gate, cleaning his lamp with a handkerchief. You can see him plainly through the glasses. In the ordinary view you see only the handkerchief rise and settle. He lifts it politely when he catches you looking.

> **The Glassmaker:** “A little fog saved a boat. Think what I could do with the whole house.”

Fenwick wants to send the visitor away as soon as the cliff door opens. The children want a say in what happens to their home. At the roof workbench Clem asks for the keys. Fenwick gives her the ring and lets the children name where they want to help. Pip volunteers to carry the rescue hook past a Haunter's count. Ada asks Agnes where to put the hand mirror. You can speak with each child and choose how to use the tools and gifts you've learned about.

The roof lets you see the three glass threads with the spectacles. The one at the front step follows a bell cord. The one at the landing goes up the lift shaft. The last pulls against the cellar grille. Each has a local clue as well: the moving bell cord, fresh scratches in the lift shaft, and the grille bolt bending toward the tide. The preparation board records what the player has seen and shows where they can install a counter.

The household can protect the house in two ways. Each has an optional trial piece to practice on before the fight.

| Route | What you prepare | What the player saw that points to it |
|---|---|---|
| **Send the gifts home** | Fit the spare reversing lens in the roof's old lighthouse mount. Make a shutter shield from the repairer's scrap. Plan a signal for Clem to shut the roof door when the beam lines up. Use the shield's bright marker or Agnes's stored bell if her button is free. | The cracked test cell sent fog back toward Ezra. The workshop diagram shows the return direction. The stand has the same lens marks as the glasses. |
| **Catch the lamp** | Fit a spring jaw to the old ballast plate and tie it to Clem's shutter rope. Use Silas to seat the iron jaw or fit a brass pawl Hettie has repaired. Set the Glassmaker's empty sample cell on the plate. | The ballast trial throws a tagged weight down the outside chute. The workshop's matching sample rack shows the Glassmaker collects his cells in order. A test with a loose cell shows his lamp pulling toward it. |

At the roof workbench, turn the beacon's village signal to warn Keeper Moss before the siege. His chart labels the signal and a lamp on the quay answers it. He brings help to the outside yard and keeps the path clear for the children. The door stays shut while you prepare. There is no real-time deadline in the rooms. When you choose to ring the dinner bell, the board tells you which defenses are ready, whether you have a way to deal with the lamp, and where you have a rescue available. You can ring while unprepared. Fenwick takes the children to their assigned stations and opens the outside shutter into the empty yard, giving the household a place to face the attack before the Glassmaker reaches the cliff door.

## The siege — Hold the line

Each approach waits for the player to inspect what is happening and commit to a response. The glasses show the thread and the target. No fast clicking is required.

| Approach | What the Haunters try | How you stop them |
|---|---|---|
| **Front step** | Pull Ezra through the closing door and siphon his fog into a glass cell. | Ezra outlines the lead Haunter in fog. Spring a cable snare on the revealed joint, or have Agnes play the stored bell sound down the marked passage and close the door on the group when they turn toward it. The lighthouse test taught that a startled Haunter turns toward a sound behind it. |
| **Upper landing** | Climb the open lift shaft to reach Agnes. | Have Clem call the loaded lift to its marked stop and block the shaft. Or move Agnes to the safe side through Ada's prepared mirror crossing so she can shut the far door while you shut the near one. |
| **Cellar** | Pry the grille from its rail and reach Hettie at the clock. | Use Silas's iron bolt on the continuous rail or lower the prepared drop-bar when the glasses show the thread cross the marked stop. |
| **Roof** | The Glassmaker brings his lamp close enough to take another gift and use it to open the house from inside. | Line up the reversing lens, raise the shield, and give Clem the cue to trap the beam on the roof. The stolen gifts travel back along their threads and the empty lamp breaks. Or let the lamp pull toward its last sample cell, then close the spring jaw and release the ballast plate. It sends the lamp down the chute to a stone catch outside the boundary. |

A missed approach lets a Haunter catch hold of a child. A prepared rescue can cut the thread before the gift leaves them. Hettie's hook works from a marked anchor. Pip can carry it past a counting Haunter if you practiced together. Ada can offer one prepared mirror crossing, either on the landing or at an approach set up for rescue. If a gift is taken, the child stays in the house. The lamp steals what they can do.

Two breaches expose the cliff door and lose the siege. One still leaves a way to reach the roof. Without a ready roof route you can discover what you were missing, then retry. A win with the reversing lens restores any gifts taken during this attempt. A Ballast win protects everybody and works even if you skipped the roof lens preparation. If one gift was already sealed in a cell, the lamp falls into the stone catch with the cell inside. The Glassmaker goes after it, but the rising tide washes the lamp out of the catch before he can reach it. In that ending the child is safe, but their gift has gone quiet. With either victory, he is forced out of the house without a working lamp or a trail. You pin the door line back into place while Keeper Moss and the islanders meet him in the yard. The player can return to preparation and attempt a rescue or the other roof route before accepting that outcome.

**Try Again.** Losing is a game checkpoint. The player can return to the dinner bell with their exact setup or to the Last Tuesday morning with their notebook and completed discoveries. Restoring an earlier state restores its materials, placements and assignments together. The notes flag the first missed counter and where to find its parts. A retry does not give Fenwick another in-story flight or create a copy of a carried object.

## Wednesday

With the lamp out of reach and the seam resealed, Fenwick takes the children to the cliff door. This time she asks them what they want to do about the loop. Clem wants to find out whether the lift will ever learn to stop in the right place. Agnes wants a morning in which Ada's laugh is a new sound. Pip wants to see if anybody remembers where he hung his coat.

They choose to let the day finish together. Fenwick leaves the clock still. The house stays a refuge on the island, and its old entrance closes behind the last visitor. The children take one ordinary step into their own Wednesday. Before you go back to the present, you help Clem and Hettie fit the hinge they've been saving for the cliff door. Hettie files a notch into the brass so Clem knows which way up it goes. In the present, the cliff frame is an ordinary door again. You recognize the notch under a century of salt and paint.

In the quieter ending, the child whose gift went silent still takes that step with everyone. They choose what to do next in their own words. The victory is about getting the children a say in their lives.

## Story rules for review

- **Boundary and dates.** In the present, the cliff door opens into the hidden 1899 stretch of coast. The house, lighthouse and nearby village share the repeated day. Fenwick, the children and the visitor remember. Villagers, the Glassmaker and Haunters return to their starting places and forget. The cliff door line and the rest of the boundary sit at the edge. Marks there survive and can be rediscovered. The first attack left a trace, and the first and second flights show it spreading. During a successful last night, resealing it stops the Glassmaker from using the hidden route again.
- **Player time.** Visit any discovered routine window before dusk. Local appointments and tide conditions change, and completed actions stay done until a full reset. Taking items, exploring and reading clues never runs a live timer.
- **Inventory.** At a full reset, the visitor keeps what they carry and any parts inside a carried assembly. Each such part's source stays empty. A part set down, spent or traded goes back to its source once it has no carried result reserving it. Repairs reset. Learned clues and relationships carry over.
- **Gifts.** The Glassmaker's lamp can draw one gift through a Haunter's glass thread. A broken thread stops a transfer. The reverse lens can send sealed gifts back while the lamp is still connected to the house. The small draw on Day Two is shown returning from the cracked cell before the second reset, so Ezra begins the last day able to help. Nobody loses their ordinary abilities or identity when a gift goes quiet.
- **Names.** Keep *Morrow Isle* as the working title. Use *Morrow House*, *the Haunters* and *the Glassmaker* after the player can see why someone would call them that. Use Fenwick and the children's names in conversations where a player can meet them through a task or a memorable action.
