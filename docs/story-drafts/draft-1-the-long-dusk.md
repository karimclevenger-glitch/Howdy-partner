## Working title

Your repo name gave me the heart of the story, so I built the plot around it.
In this draft, **"Tap-tap, chicka boom boom"** is a hand-clapping rhyme two girls made up fifty years ago,
and the whole ending depends on it.

Title options (pick one, mix them, or give me your own):

1. **Tap Tap Chicka Boom Boom** (playful, and it's the finale's title too)
2. **Beatrix and the Long Dusk** (sounds like a classic fairy-tale storybook)
3. **The Long Dusk: A Tale of the Gloaming**

## Logline

When a mysterious music box pulls seventeen-year-old **Princess Tamsin** into the Gloaming, a fairy-tale
realm stuck in an endless evening, her seventy-two-year-old grandmother **Beatrix the Bold**, a retired
legendary adventurer with bad knees and a worse temper, grabs her cane and goes in after her. There she
finds out the Gloaming is ruled by the best friend she left behind fifty years ago. That friend
hasn't aged a day, and she plans to pull the whole waking world into a dusk that never ends.

## What it's really about

- **Endings are what make things precious.** The villain's plan is "nothing ever ends, so nobody ever
  gets left behind." It sounds lovely, and it's a trap.
- **Growing up and growing old are both kinds of leaving.** Tamsin is scared of tomorrow and Beatrix is
  scared of yesterday. They each have to face their own fear.
- **Friendship survives distance if someone remembers the rhyme.**

## Tone

A storybook fairy tale with a mischievous streak. It should be **funny most of the time** (a dry narrator,
a grandmother with no filter, absurd creatures, ridiculous deaths), **a little spooky in places**, and
**genuinely moving at the end**. It's PG: cartoon peril, nothing gory, and every death is a joke
with an instant "Try Again."

---

## The world: the Gloaming

The Gloaming is the land of **evening**, the twilight country between waking and dreaming where fairy-tale
things live. Normally its evening flows like any other. Golden hour turns to sunset, then blue hour, then night,
then midnight, and at dawn the **Dawn Lark** sings the sun up. For the length of that one song, the
**Sunrise Gate** between the Gloaming and our world opens.

Fifty years ago, the evening **stopped**. Every corner of the Gloaming froze at whatever moment it was in,
so each region is now stuck at a different hour. That gives every chapter its own look and color palette,
and the game gets darker hour by hour until the finale finally reaches dawn:

| Chapter | Region | Stuck at… | Palette | Played by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Larkspire Castle (our world) | a summer evening | warm cream, rose, green | Tamsin |
| 1 | **The Glasswater**: a sea that froze into glass mid-storm | golden hour | amber, honey, sea-glass teal | Tamsin |
| 2 | **The Ember Wood**: autumn leaves that never fall | sunset | ember orange, crimson, plum | Beatrix |
| 3 | **The Rootdeep**: star-mines under the forest | no time at all (underground) | indigo, starlight white, Tamsin's gold glow | Tamsin |
| 4 | **Hushwater**: a stilt-village marsh whose shadows walked away | blue hour | cobalt, teal, lamplight yellow | Beatrix |
| 5 | **The Clockwork Observatory**: where the minutes are kept | the last minute before midnight | navy, silver, brass | Both (alternating) |
| 6 | **Vesper Keep**: the Dusk Queen's castle, built from captured sunsets | the stopped heart of it all → **dawn** | violet and rose, breaking into gold and pink | Both |

## The backstory (the secret at the center)

Fifty years ago, two young women were the terrors of the kingdom of Larkspire: **Bea** and **Cel**, best
friends since they were six, the kind who finish each other's sentences and start each other's fights.
As little girls they made up a clapping rhyme, set to the song the lark sang outside Bea's window every morning.

At twenty-two they snuck into the Gloaming on the greatest dare of their lives, to pluck a golden feather
from the Dawn Lark itself. A Lark feather grants one wish. They got it. At dawn the Lark sang, the Sunrise
Gate opened for exactly one song's length, and they ran.

**Cel dropped the feather.** She turned back for it, because she'd planned to give Bea the wish.
Bea was already at the gate. She heard Cel shouting her name. The song was ending.

**Bea went through. The gate closed.**

On the far side, Cel clutched the feather and made her wish through tears: *"Let nothing ever end."*

The wish came true the way wishes do in fairy tales, which is to say horribly. The Gloaming's evening stopped
dead. The Lark fell silent, because you can't sing a dawn that never comes, and Cel caged it to keep it
from ever leaving. Since time no longer passes in the Gloaming, Cel never aged. She slowly became
**the Dusk Queen**.

What she never knew is that **Bea came back.** Every single dawn for ten years, Bea stood in the meadow
where the gate used to open, with her pack on, waiting. It never opened again, because Cel's wish meant
the Gloaming never had another dawn. Eventually Bea married, raised a son, became a queen, and grew old.
She told her adventure stories to her granddaughter a thousand times, and she always left that part out.

Cel spent those fifty years watching Bea through an enchanted mirror. She saw Bea's hair go grey, and she
saw her start to need a cane. Now she's terrified of the one ending she can't stop from inside the Gloaming,
which is Bea's. So she has built a spell to stretch the endless evening over the waking world too.
**No one will ever grow old. No one will ever leave. No one will ever be left behind again.**

The last ingredient she needs is a willing heart from the Larkspire royal line: someone who *chooses* to
give up their tomorrow. Tamsin, the night before she has to start training to be queen, would love
nothing more than to never grow up.

### The rhyme

> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Lark's asleep in the evening gloom.*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom bee,*
> *Who's gonna wake her? You and me!*
> *Over the hill and over the sea,*
> *Wherever you go, you'll come back to me.*
> *Tap-tap, chicka boom boom,*
> *Up comes the sun! Make room, make room!*

Beatrix taught it to Tamsin when Tamsin was small. The music box plays its melody. And in the finale,
**the player taps it out on the screen** as a gentle rhythm sequence (more on that in Chapter 6).

## The cast

### The heroines

**Princess Tamsin of Larkspire** (17)
Grew up on her grandmother's adventure stories and privately believes they're exaggerated. (They're
actually understated.) She's brave, curious, a chatterbox, and a know-it-all from books ("I *read* about
this!"). She's quietly dreading tomorrow, when she starts her Heir's Year of ledgers, treaties and
ribbon-cuttings.
*Signature tool:* her **sketchbook and charcoal**. Tamsin can sketch things to copy and carry them:
a symbol on a wall, a map, a face, a lock pattern. She can show the sketch to someone or use it somewhere else.
*Arc:* from wanting adventure as a way to escape growing up, to choosing to grow up.

**Beatrix the Bold, Dowager Queen of Larkspire ("Granny Trix")** (72)
A legend in her day. Today she has bad knees, a brass ear-trumpet she insists she doesn't need, a carved
cane, and absolutely no patience for nonsense. She's sharp, very funny, and secretly sentimental. Anyone in the
Gloaming old enough remembers her, some fondly and most not, because she owes a fox fifty years of bar tab and she
once stole a goose.
*Signature tools:* her **cane**, for hooking, reaching, poking and tapping (tap-tap), and her **ear-trumpet**,
for eavesdropping. Her **reputation** matters too: some puzzles are solved by being recognized,
and others by *not* being recognized.
*Arc:* fifty years of carrying the moment she didn't go back. She has to face it.

### The villain

**Celandine, the Dusk Queen** (looks 22, is 72)
Bea's best friend. A celandine is a little yellow flower that **closes at dusk**, and she wears one.
She's elegant, witty, warm when she wants to be, and frightening when she doesn't. Her magic makes things
**stay**: frozen moments, stopped clocks, kept memories. She isn't evil for the fun of it. She's love that
curdled into control, and the finale only works if the player half-understands her.
In Tamsin's chapters she appears in disguise as **Linnet**, a scruffy, funny young adventurer who
befriends Tamsin and helps her. The player gets clues before Tamsin does.

### Companions (who double as the hint system)

**Admiral Pinch**, a hermit crab who uses a brass ship's lantern as his shell. He believes he's the last
admiral of a navy that turned to glass. He's pompous, loyal, and cowardly in a brave sort of way, and he joins
Tamsin in Chapter 1. Tap him for a hint. He can also be *used* as a tool, because claws pry things.

**Dimity**, a small, anxious moth with very big opinions about light. She latches onto Beatrix's lantern
in Chapter 2. She has lived her whole life in an evening that never ends and has never once seen a
sunrise. Tap her for a hint. In Chapter 4, put her in front of the lantern and her shadow becomes
enormous. Her arc ends with the first dawn she's ever seen.

### The running gag

**Gertrude the Goose.** Fifty years ago, Beatrix stole her (long story). Since nothing ages in the Gloaming,
Gertrude is still here, still furious, and turns up in every Beatrix chapter trying to get revenge.
In the finale she ends up being the one who carries Beatrix to the Dusk Queen's castle.
"This doesn't make us even."

## Chapter by chapter

Each chapter below gives the setup, the main story beats, the kind of puzzles it has, and a few sample deaths.
Exact puzzles get designed in detail chapter by chapter once you approve the story (see "What happens next"
at the bottom). Chapter 1 is fleshed out further because I'm proposing it as our first playable slice.

### Prologue: "Seventeen Candles"
*Larkspire Castle, a summer evening. Playable tutorial: a few rooms.*

It's Tamsin's 17th birthday. Tomorrow her Heir's Year starts and she's dreading it. Granny Trix gives
her a gift, her own battered old adventuring satchel ("Every story I ever told you started with this bag").
Then another gift turns up with no name on it: a **music box shaped like a setting sun**.
At sunset, on her balcony, Tamsin winds it. It plays a melody she knows from somewhere. The sunset light
pours out of the box, the balcony folds away like the page of a pop-up book, and Tamsin is gone.

Down in the rose garden, Beatrix hears the melody and goes white. She knows exactly whose music box that is.
She hobbles up the tower stairs, smashes the glass case labelled *"The Lantern of Beatrix the Bold:
DO NOT TOUCH"*, grabs her cane, and jumps into the closing light.
*"Oh, my knees are going to hate this."*
The light splits in two. Tamsin falls one way and Beatrix the other.

*Teaches:* tapping to walk and interact, the inventory, the sketchbook, and talking to people.

### Chapter 1: "The Sea That Stood Still" (Tamsin)
*The Glasswater, frozen at golden hour.*

Tamsin lands on top of a giant **wave made of amber glass**. Around her is a whole stormy sea that
froze mid-crash. A galleon is stuck halfway through sinking, a dark lighthouse stands on a glass reef,
and the shore is a long way off at the bottom of the **Long Wave**, a great curling slope of glass that
ends in a field of jagged glass spikes. At the bottom sits Mr. Gullingsworth's customs buoy, and he will not
let *anyone* proceed shoreward without a stamped Lost Permit.

**Chapter goal:** get down the Long Wave to the shore. The draft puzzle web looks like this. It has several
threads you can work on in any order, which matters so the player never feels stuck on one thing:

```
GOAL: Slide down the Long Wave and reach the shore
│
├─ A SLED ──────────── the big copper serving platter in the galleon's galley
│    └─ Admiral Pinch won't let anyone loot "his flagship"
│         └─ he'll enlist you (and give up the platter) if you look after his dignity:
│              an admiral needs a HAT
│                └─ fold a spare Form L-0-5-T (Gullingsworth hands those out freely) into a bicorne
│                   → Pinch joins you as companion
│
├─ A STAMPED LOST PERMIT ── from Mr. Gullingsworth
│    ├─ the form needs a PORTRAIT of the applicant
│    │    └─ Tamsin can sketch herself, but the glass sea's reflection makes her look like
│    │       "an alarmed haddock" → she needs a true mirror → Perla's hand-mirror
│    └─ his ink-pad is dry (nothing liquid has existed here for 50 years)
│         └─ INK = Tamsin's charcoal + the only liquid for miles: one of Perla's happy tears
│
└─ THE SPIKES at the bottom must be smoothed
     └─ light the LIGHTHOUSE. Its beam makes glass "remember it's water" for a moment.
          ├─ the crank is frozen into the reef → use Admiral Pinch's claws to pry it loose
          ├─ the lamp needs a spark → flint striker from the galleon's cannon
          ├─ aim it using the old keeper's LOGBOOK (bearings written in the margins)
          │    → aim 1: at Perla, softening the glass around her. She's free! She weeps happy
          │      tears (catch one in a seashell) and gives you her mirror.
          └─  → aim 2: at the spikes, melting them smooth
```

The finale is a short, forgiving sliding sequence down the Long Wave on a serving platter with a crab
screaming in your satchel. At the bottom, Tamsin is waved through ("Approved!"). On the beach, a young
woman with a feathered cap is sitting on a rock, sketching. She has a little yellow flower pinned to her coat.
*"You came down the Long Wave? On a* platter*? Oh, I like you already. I'm Linnet."*

**Sample deaths** (each one plays a short gag, gets a narrator line, then "Try Again"):
- **Lick the glass sea.** Your tongue joins the sea, permanently. *"A lesson most people learn from lamp-posts."*
- **Stare into the lit lighthouse beam.** *"Tamsin remembered that she was mostly water. The rest of her didn't get a vote."* (This one also teaches you the beam's rule.)
- **Slide down before the spikes are smoothed** and Tamsin shatters into a kaleidoscope of a thousand tiny Tamsins, all complaining at once.
- **Slide down without a permit** and Mr. Gullingsworth files you. In a cabinet. Under L, forever.
- **Poke the Glass Leviathan in the eye.** It wakes up for exactly one second. That's enough.

### Chapter 2: "Old Debts in the Ember Wood" (Beatrix)
*The Ember Wood, frozen at sunset.*

Beatrix lands on a bed of leaves hanging motionless in midair, because nothing here has ever been allowed
to finish falling. She needs two things: **where Tamsin is**, and **who did this**. The only creatures who
know everything are the crows of the **Gossip Exchange**, and they trade only in secrets. Beatrix has seventy-two
years of excellent gossip, but it's all fifty years stale.

**Main threads:**
- **Fresh secrets for the crows.** Beatrix eavesdrops with her **ear-trumpet** around the wood to learn
  that Madame Vulpine waters down the cider, that Hewitt the woodcutter is afraid of the dark, and that
  Gertrude's "golden" eggs are painted.
- **Her old map.** She pawned it behind Madame Vulpine's bar fifty years ago. The tab must be paid, and
  Vulpine's hearth has gone cold, so Beatrix pays in glowing sunwood firewood earned by helping Hewitt.
- **Dimity.** The little moth is trapped inside a jar at the inn, being used as a nightlight. Beatrix frees her
  and gets a companion.
- **Gertrude.** She ambushes Beatrix whenever she strays near the pond. Beatrix needs a distraction to get
  past (it's comedy, but it's also a puzzle).

**Ending beat:** The crows finally tell her: the one who stopped the evening is a queen who wears "the little
yellow flower that shuts at dusk." Beatrix goes very quiet.
*Narrator: "Beatrix did not say anything for a long while. Which, for Beatrix, was a very long while indeed."*

**Sample deaths:** insult the crows and they spread a rumor that you're dead. In the Ember Wood, gossip is
legally binding. Eat the sunset-berries and you turn into a sunset, which is lovely to look at but impractical.
Sit on the comfy toadstool and it turns out to be a toad's hat. The toad sits on you.

### Chapter 3: "Down Among the Star-Miners" (Tamsin)
*The Rootdeep, beneath the Ember Wood.*

Linnet knows a shortcut to the Dusk Queen's castle: *"Under, not over."* She takes Tamsin and Pinch into
the Rootdeep and then slips off to "scout ahead." Down here the near-blind mole-folk are being forced by
the Hushers to mine **fallen stars**, and the starlight is being shipped up to power something in the
Dusk Queen's castle.

**The transformation:** Tamsin catches a falling star-crumb and it sinks into her palm. **She glows.**
The mole-folk decide she's the Lamp-Bearer from their prophecy, which is very flattering and very
inconvenient. Her glow becomes a tool: it lights up dark tunnels and blinds Hushers. But it also attracts the
**Gloomworm**, which eats light, so she has to learn when to cover herself up.

**Main threads:** win over Nan Delve, the real boss of the moles. Trick the Echo into saying a password
it never would. Survive Wendell's poetry. Sabotage the starlight mine-cart line. A tunnel collapse and a
daring rescue by Linnet make Tamsin trust her completely.

**Ending beat:** Resting at the top of a mineshaft, Tamsin absent-mindedly claps the old rhyme her Granny
taught her. Linnet goes still. *"Who taught you that?"* "My Granny. She said she made it up with her best
friend when she was little." Linnet looks away and doesn't say anything for a moment.

**Sample deaths:** shout in the Echo's cave and the Echo shouts back much louder, which brings the ceiling down.
Glow too brightly in the Gloomworm's tunnel. Listen to Wendell's epic poem all the way to the end.
*"Some deaths are slow."*

### Chapter 4: "Where the Shadows Went" (Beatrix)
*Hushwater, frozen at blue hour.*

Hushwater is a lantern-lit village of frog-folk built on stilts over perfectly still water. **Nobody here has a
shadow.** Fifty years of endless sunset stretched the shadows long and restless, and when the Dusk Queen
promised them they'd never have to follow anyone again, they left. Now they live in **Umbra**, a town
that exists only in the water's reflection.

The moment Beatrix arrives, **her own shadow peels off and walks away.** It looks like Bea at twenty-two:
the legendary Beatrix the Bold, cocky and fast, with knees that work. Old Stilt the ferryman will only
carry passengers who are *whole*, and the only way onward is his ferry.

**The mechanic:** **shadow puppetry.** With her lantern and the objects in her inventory, Beatrix casts
shapes on walls to talk to shadows, fool them, and frighten them. Put Dimity in front of the lantern and her
shadow is the size of a dragon.

**The heart of Beatrix's story:** Widow Pipkin kept the room Bea and Cel stayed in fifty years ago exactly as
it was. Things in that room (an old journal, two cups, a ribbon) piece together the truth. The Shadow
won't come back until Beatrix says out loud the thing she's never told anyone:
*"I heard her calling me. And I didn't go back."*

**The temptation:** her Shadow offers her a deal. *"Stay down here with me. Be me again."* If you accept, you get
a quiet, sad "ending" ("…and Beatrix was young forever, and never saw Tamsin grow up"), then Try Again.

**Ending beat:** Whole again, Beatrix reaches the foot of the Starloft stairs. Far above, two figures are
climbing: Tamsin, and a young woman Beatrix hasn't seen in fifty years.
*"…Cel?"*

**Sample deaths:** fall into the still water and your reflection keeps you. Pay Old Stilt with a bad song and
he tips you overboard. Wander into Umbra without a shadow disguise and the shadows make you one of them.

### Chapter 5: "The Last Minute Before Midnight" (both, alternating)
*The Clockwork Observatory at the top of the Starloft.*

The Dusk Queen's castle, **Vesper Keep**, floats beyond the great clock and appears only at the last minute
before midnight. Her Hushers stole that minute from Mother Minute's jars. You **switch between Tamsin and
Beatrix**, who are on different floors of the tower and can't reach each other, and they help each other
without knowing it. A lever Tamsin pulls opens Beatrix's path, a loose minute Beatrix catches
restarts Tamsin's gears, and so on. **Loose minutes** flit around the tower like fireflies.

**The twist lands here.** The two of them finally meet at the clock face. Beatrix sees Linnet and goes for her:
*"Tamsin, get away from her!"* Linnet lets the disguise fall and becomes the Dusk Queen.
Celandine turns to Tamsin. *"Ask her. Ask your grandmother what she did at the Sunrise Gate."*
Beatrix can't deny it.

Tamsin has heard a thousand stories about how brave her grandmother was, and *nobody ever told her this one*.
Hurt and furious, **she chooses to go with Celandine.** The clock strikes the last minute, the Keep
appears, and they're gone. Beatrix is left alone on the clock face with a moth on her shoulder.
*"Well. Nobody ever said the stories had happy middles."*

### Chapter 6: "Tap Tap Chicka Boom Boom" (both, alternating)
*Vesper Keep: from frozen dusk to dawn.*

**Tamsin, inside the Keep,** is the Queen's honored guest. She explores the **Halls of Kept Moments**,
galleries of frozen memories. She sees Bea and Cel at six years old making up the rhyme, she sees their
adventures, and she sees the night at the Sunrise Gate *from Cel's side*. Then she finds Celandine's mirror and
watches Larkspire slowly freezing, with her own parents stopped mid-supper. She finally understands what
"forever" costs. She finds the **Dawn Lark** caged in the heart of the Great Lantern and frees it, but after
fifty years of silence it has forgotten its song.

**Beatrix, outside,** has to break into a castle in the sky. She calls in every favor she's earned. The
friends from every chapter return for one last push, ending with Gertrude the Goose carrying her up on
her back. *"This doesn't make us even."*

**The confrontation** happens atop the Great Lantern. Celandine asks Tamsin for her *yes*: stay seventeen
forever, no crown, no goodbyes. **Choosing yes** gives a bittersweet false ending and Try Again. **Tamsin says no.**
Then Beatrix arrives and tells Cel the truth she never knew: *"I came back, Cel. Every morning for ten years.
Until Gideon hid my boots."*

Beatrix starts the clapping rhyme. Tamsin joins in. **The player taps the rhythm on screen.** It's a short,
gentle, forgiving sequence with the two women's hands clapping on screen. Celandine resists… and then her
hands start to move. The three of them finish it together: *"Up comes the sun! Make room, make room!"*

The Lark remembers its song, and it sings. **Dawn breaks** over the Gloaming for the first time in fifty years.
In the light, Celandine finally ages all fifty years in a single shimmering moment, until she and Bea are
two old women looking at each other.
*"Look at us. Two old biddies."* / *"Speak for yourself."*

Everything frozen starts moving again. The glass sea crashes back into water, Admiral Pinch's navy
sails, Perla swims, the leaves finally fall, the shadows come home, and the minutes tick. Dimity sees her very first
sunrise. Gertrude gets her revenge with one solid peck, and then they're square.

### Epilogue: "Tomorrow"
Larkspire, in morning light. Tamsin, wearing a slightly crooked circlet, begins her Heir's Year. She's chosen to
grow up. In the front row, two old women heckle the ceremony. Later, on a balcony, they play the clapping
game again, slowly, laughing whenever they miss.
**The End.**

## Optional extra: Lark Feathers

There are three hidden golden feathers per chapter. Each one unlocks a short illustrated memory of Bea
and Cel's past adventures, which adds the backstory piece by piece for players who go looking. It's
completely optional and never needed to finish the game. (Cut it if it's too much.)

## How this echoes the games you loved, and where it's ours

**The elements you asked for, kept:** a fairy-tale realm made of very different regions · two heroines who are
family, separated by magic, in alternating chapters, who meet up for the finale · a villain from the past with
a world-ending magic scheme · strange creatures and a lot of personality · a transformation (Tamsin's glow,
Beatrix's shadow) · lots of comic deaths with an instant Try Again · inventory puzzles with whimsical logic ·
storybook chapter titles · a narrator with a dry sense of humor.

**What's new and ours:** a 72-year-old hero who's funny and capable · a villain you end up feeling for, whose
plan grows out of love · a deceptive friend whose identity the player can work out before the heroine does ·
"temptation endings" where a death is actually a meaningful choice · an evening that gets darker chapter
by chapter until it finally reaches dawn · a finale built on a rhythm you tap on a phone screen · a theme
about aging that a grown-up who played adventure games as a kid will feel.

**What we're deliberately not borrowing:** no characters, names, places, plot, puzzles, jokes, dialogue,
art or music from King's Quest VII or any other game.

## Questions for you about the story

1. **Gut reaction overall:** is this the kind of story you want to spend months inside?
2. **Grandmother and granddaughter.** Do you love this pairing, or would you rather a different family pair
   (mother/daughter, sisters, father/daughter)?
3. **The ending's emotion.** I've aimed for something that might make you a little teary. Is that what you want,
   or would you rather keep it lighter?
4. **How the heroines look.** Hair, skin tone, build, clothing: do you have a picture in your head? (I put draft
   descriptions in the art tests, but it's your call.)
5. **Anything you specifically loved as a kid** in that style of game (a kind of puzzle, a type of scene, a mood)
   that you want to make sure has a modern, original equivalent here?
6. **Length.** As drafted, this is roughly 45–50 scenes over a prologue + 6 chapters, probably 6–10 hours of play.
   Is that the size you're picturing?
7. **Title?**
