# Batch 001: Art style tests

**For ChatGPT:** read [`README.md`](README.md) in this folder first. Do these tests **one at a time**, show each
result to the owner, and ask what they think before moving on.

**For the owner:** these are experiments, not final art. They use the *world* of the current story draft (an old house on
an island, 1899) but no specific characters, so nothing is wasted if the details change. Paste the results you like (and the ones you hate) into
your chat with Claude, with your gut reaction. "I don't know why, but I love this one" is genuinely useful feedback.

---

## The three styles, in plain words

**A. Painted Storybook.** It looks like a classic hand-drawn animated fairy-tale film. Backgrounds are soft, glowing paintings
with visible brushstrokes. The characters are crisper, with clean outlines and flat colors, so they pop off the painting like
cartoon characters standing inside a painted world. *(Picture the castle-and-forest backgrounds of the old animated fairy-tale
movies.)* This is the closest to the feel of 90s fairy-tale adventure games, and it's **Claude's recommendation**: outlined,
flat-colored characters are the easiest for AI to keep consistent and the easiest to animate.

**B. Paper Theatre.** Everything looks handmade from cut paper and card, layered like a pop-up book or a shoebox diorama.
You see paper texture, cut edges, and little shadows between the layers. Characters look like paper puppets.
*(Picture a pop-up book come to life.)* It's cozy and crafty, and puppet-style animation looks completely intentional in it.

**C. Ink & Watercolor.** It looks like the illustrations in a classic children's book: loose pen lines, soft see-through
watercolor washes, and lots of light and air. *(Picture an old-fashioned illustrated bedtime-story book.)* It's gentle and charming,
but less dramatic in dark or spooky scenes, and the sketchy lines are harder to animate cleanly.
